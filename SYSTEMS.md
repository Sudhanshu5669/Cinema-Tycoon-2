| 5  | Camera (follow, deadzone, pixel-snapped to avoid shimmer) | done | `src/game/camera.js`. **Phaser's own `startFollow` is deliberately not used.** With `roundPixels` on, Phaser floors the lerped scroll and feeds that back as next frame's scroll, so the fraction is lost every frame and a slow lerp can never close the last pixel — the camera stalls until the target is several pixels past the deadzone and keeps that error when the player stops. Here the float scroll is kept in `FollowCamera` and only a rounded copy reaches the camera, so the smoothing converges on exact numbers while the renderer only ever sees integers. (`startFollow` also silently resets `roundPixels` to false unless you pass it — a second reason to own this.) Smoothing is an exponential rate per second, not a per-frame lerp, so the feel does not change with frame rate; the camera aims at mid-body rather than the feet. The dev room grew to 3x3 screens (a camera has nothing to prove in a single-screen room) and the deadzone is outlined on screen in DEV builds. Verified in real Chromium: `node tools/smoke.mjs`, 27/27, adding centring at rest, deadzone hold, follow past the deadzone, integer scroll on every sampled frame, the player never leaving the screen, and the clamp at both world corners. `__dev.warp` was added so the bounds check does not have to walk 960px in real time. |
| 6  | Tile renderer + 3/4 perspective wall faces | done | `src/game/tilemap/`. Built with an **elevation model** from the start, per user direction ("versatile enough to have levels"): a map carries a `height` grid (integer terrain elevation, 0 = street) plus ordered `layers` with roles `ground`/`flat`/`object`/`overhead`, and `buildings`/`platforms` sugar the renderer expands. A cell's top surface is drawn `e * STEP` px up (`STEP` = one tile, in `projection.js`, a renderer knob not a locked art constant); the vertical drop to lower ground in front is drawn as an **oblique face projecting straight down** — no horizontal skew, so it stays compatible with a 4-facing character. **Buildings are a roof cap + a composed storeyed face** (cornice / wall / ground-floor material / plinth, `faceRowPlan`) with windows and a door placed in face space, plus a cast shadow on the pavement — this is what un-flattens the old straight-on-facade look. Everything static is baked into offscreen **canvas textures** (`atlas.js` `bake`), not RenderTextures: painting a 1920px ground plane cell-by-cell into a RenderTexture stalls for seconds on GPU readback, a 2D canvas does it in ms and the result is one static image with no per-frame cost. Depth sort is by world-Y of ground contact: the player's feet (`player.js` sets `sprite.depth`) vs each structure's front-wall line, so the player is behind a building unless their feet are south of it; roof and face share that depth. `heightAt`/`solidAt` are exposed for collision (#7), which now builds on them. **Elevated surfaces catch shadows too**: a shadow's hull is computed in ground coordinates, so it paints straight onto the flat street, but a roof cap or a platform top is drawn shifted (and, for a foreshortened roof, squashed) relative to that same footprint — a hull painted only on the ground can never visually land on one, since the elevated surface's own image, at a much higher depth, just sits in front of it. Every roof cap and platform top gets its own small baked layer: any *other* caster's hull is clipped to that surface's footprint (Sutherland-Hodgman against the footprint rect, `clipToRect` in `shadows.js`) then carried through the same footprint -> screen transform the surface's own top image uses, so a tall neighbour's shadow correctly darkens a shorter roof or a nearby raised platform; a surface excludes its own caster index, or it would always contain its own unshifted footprint and paint itself solid black every frame. A clip along two footprints' shared edge degrades to a zero-area sliver rather than a real overlap, so the hit test is gated on polygon area (shoelace), not just point count — a raw `length >= 3` check flagged every contiguous building pair as shadowed at every hour, noon included. Art is deliberately still the SYSTEMS #0 placeholder tiles (`art/flat/tiles.mjs`), pending an "art second" pass. Atlas built by `node tools/build-tiles-sheet.mjs` -> `public/assets/tiles.{png,json}` (added to `npm run build:art`); `tools/build-tiles.mjs` is the separate static review sheet. Dev map is `public/assets/city.json` (a row of buildings, a kerbed road, one raised grass platform at elevation 2, streetlamps, one object + one overhead cell) since SYSTEMS #9 replaced the original hardcoded `devmap.js`. **Cast-shadow layer** (`shadows.js` + `sun.js`): every raised caster (building or platform) drops a shadow on the street whose **length scales with its silhouette height** and whose **length, direction and opacity track an hour-of-day**. `sun.js` is a deliberately cheated sun — a physical one spends midday south of the street throwing shadows north, away from the camera and behind the buildings, so this one is kept low and behind the viewer all day: shadows always rake south onto the visible pavement, swinging south-west in the morning to near-straight-down and short at noon to south-east (and long) in the evening, and fading out between dusk and dawn. Evening agrees with the west-lit flat tiles; the morning half-hour disagrees, which is a job for the #8 light pass. Each shadow is the convex hull of the caster's ground footprint swept along the sun offset; all of them are painted solid black into one throttled offscreen canvas (~0.05h buckets) and shown as a single Image whose alpha is the hour's shadow opacity — baking a flat mask and fading it once is what stops contiguous buildings' shadows stacking into double-dark seams. The hour is just a settable value here (`__dev.setTime` / `__dev.autoTime`, HUD `time` line, `DevScene.START_HOUR`) — plus dev-only hotkeys (`DevScene.bindDevHotkeys`, raw `keydown`, gated on `import.meta.env.DEV` the same as `__dev` itself) for testing without a console open: `1`-`6` jump to dawn/morning/noon/afternoon/dusk/night, `[`/`]` step an hour, `T` toggles auto-advance; the HUD prints the binding so it doesn't have to be remembered; the real day/night clock is #16 and the coloured day/evening overlay is #8. Verified in real Chromium: `node tools/smoke.mjs` — adds map-covers-world, `heightAt`/`solidAt` contract inside a building vs open road, player sorting behind vs in front of a building's front wall, the platform face sitting below its raised top, the shadow model (longer near dusk than noon, proportional to height, west in the morning / east in the evening, none at night, layer opacity firmer at midday than dusk and off at night), and a taller neighbour's long dawn shadow reaching a shorter roof with nothing showing at noon; walk/camera checks still green with the renderer live and a clean console. **Follow-up from user request ("light interacting with me as a player and creating shadows")**: the player now casts a shadow too, from the sun (this system) and from nearby point lights (#8's windows/marquee/streetlamps). Deliberately its own small canvas (`ShadowLayer.updatePlayer`, `PLAYER_SHADOW_REACH` around the player), not folded into the world-sized static bake above -- the player moves every frame, buildings/platforms/streetlamps never do, and redoing that whole unmoving bake on every step would be pure waste. Reuses the existing sprite-silhouette shear technique unchanged (the player is exactly the same "thin, irregular caster" case a streetlamp already was) for the sun shadow; a *point*-light shadow is the same shear math with a different (dx,dy) -- normalized direction away from the light, scaled by proximity (`PLIGHT_MIN_LEN`..`PLIGHT_MAX_LEN`, both length and darkness fading together as the player nears a light's own edge) -- since unlike the sun, a light has a position, so the direction is different every time the player moves relative to it. `LightingLayer#shadowSources` (#8) reports the nearest lit lights actually close enough to be lighting the player at all (capped at 2, so more than one nearby lamp can throw its own separate shadow, same as real point-source light); asking it directly, rather than re-deriving a light's exact position by hand, matters because a streetlamp's *bulb* sits well above its map placement (near the top of the pole) -- the smoke test hit this itself as a bug in the test, not the system, when it first guessed that offset instead of asking. Bucketed on player position + frame + hour + light state so an idle player under a still sky redraws only on its own idle-blink frame change. Verified in real Chromium: `node tools/smoke.mjs` -- the player's shadow reads along the sun's own direction and not the opposite way, and away from a nearby streetlamp rather than toward it (sampling a small spread of distances along each direction and taking the max, since the character's own silhouette has real gaps -- the legs -- that a single exact-pixel sample can land inside of and misread as unlit); `node tools/smoke.mjs`'s existing walk-speed real-time check (the same canary that caught the `maxLights` and Bloom regressions earlier) stayed green across repeated runs, confirming the per-frame small-canvas redraw is cheap enough. |
| 7  | Collision (tile-based, per-tile solid flags) | done | `src/game/collision.js`, wired into `player.js`. Resolves a proposed move against the renderer's `solidAt` one axis at a time — trying the full diagonal move first would reject the whole step the instant either component alone was blocked, which reads as snagging on a corner rather than sliding past it. The footprint is a small box at the feet (`COLLIDER_W`/`COLLIDER_H` in `config.js`, both under one tile), not the full sprite: a three-tile-tall character's shoulders should be free to visually overlap a wall while the feet still stop at it. Sampling just the box's four corners is enough to catch every solid tile it overlaps, since a box under one tile in each dimension can straddle at most a 2x2 group of cells and each of those must contain at least one corner. `Player` takes an optional `solidAt` query (omitted, movement is unobstructed, which is what the pure walk checks want) and resolves collision internally so its own position/sprite-sync stays the single source of truth; DevScene passes `map.solidAt` and keeps a plain world-edge clamp on top, since `solidAt` reports false outside the map. Platforms are deliberately not solid — only buildings block, matching the renderer's own `_bakeSolid`. Verified in real Chromium: `node tools/smoke.mjs` — walking straight into the cinema's front wall stops the player short of it and never inside its solid footprint, holding position under continued pressure, and a diagonal held against the wall slides along it (the perpendicular axis keeps advancing) instead of snagging. |
| 8  | Lighting layer (colored overlay + light cutouts) | done | `src/game/lighting.js` + `tilemap/sun.js`. **Real per-pixel WebGL shader, not a baked overlay** — a deliberate user call, made explicitly to buy future headroom ("in the future we want to add a lot of pretty things... make sure the system is dynamic and robust"): this wires Phaser's own `Light2D` pipeline (`node_modules/phaser/.../shaders/src/Light.frag` — ambient colour plus per-fragment falloff over N colour point lights), not a hand-rolled one, so growing the look later (more lights, a flickering torch, a lantern that follows the player into an interior) is a data change, never new GLSL. No normal maps are bound, so every surface reads flat-facing-camera (Phaser's own default `__NORMAL` texture) — consistent with this project's no-shading-pass flat art; a light still falls off and tints with colour, it just never fakes bump detail into flat pixel art. **Light sources are derived, not authored twice**: `TileMapRenderer._buildBuilding` turns every `window` facade tile and each building's `awning` into a world-space light point as it already computes their face-space -> world position for the art itself, pushed into `map.lights`'s underlying list; a new kind of glowing thing is one `GLOW_CURVES` entry in `sun.js` plus one pushed point, nothing Phaser-side changes. **Ambient colour and every light's colour/intensity are pure functions of the hour** (`ambientFor`, `glowFor` in `sun.js`, alongside the existing `shadowFor`) — night is dark cool-blue, day is bright and near-neutral ("as authored"), dusk is a warm-magenta transitional tone that gives the marquee's warm glow something dim to punch through; ordinary windows switch on approaching dusk and off after dawn (a sine hump mirroring `shadowFor`'s own day-hump, just centred on midnight instead of noon), and the cinema marquee — deliberately the street's one saturated colour accent, echoing the character style rule "exactly one saturated accent" — is on its own earlier/brighter timer, like a sign on a switch rather than a resident's lamp. This is also what resolves the morning-shadow mismatch #6 flagged as this system's job: the ambient dawn ramp now agrees with the west-lit tiles where the cast shadow alone did not. Every image the renderer places (`_place`, plus the loose object/overhead images `_buildLoose` places directly) and the player sprite (`player.js`) opt into the pipeline via `wireLight()`, a no-op under the Canvas renderer so a WebGL-less fallback degrades to "no dynamic lighting" instead of throwing. **`render.maxLights` (main.js) governs on-screen shader cost, not total light count** — Phaser's `LightsManager` culls to the nearest `maxLights` lights actually within camera view each frame regardless of how many exist across the whole map, but the fragment shader's loop bound is a compile-time constant sized from that same config value, so it is *not* "set it to the total number of lights you'll ever have": raising it from the default 10 to 64 (one blanket guess at "plenty of headroom") measurably cut the game's real-time frame pacing in about half and broke wall-clock-timed walk-speed checks; 16 (a real ~2x margin over what's actually visible in one screen of this dev map, ~8-10 lights) restores full speed. Verified in real Chromium: `node tools/smoke.mjs` — pure-function checks on `ambientFor`/`glowFor` mirroring how `shadowFor` itself is tested (noon brighter than midnight, windows unlit by day and lit by night, the marquee provably switching on before ordinary windows do), then live checks that the `Light2D` pipeline is actually engaged (not a silent Canvas degrade), the live ambient colour and window/marquee glow track the same hour the shadow layer already uses, and walk/camera/collision/shadow checks are all still green with the shader live and a clean console. **Follow-up from user review**: lights read as flat, crisply-bordered discs, not atmosphere. Researched rather than guessed — [ordered dithering for pixel-art gradients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_dithering) and [Phaser's FX system](https://docs.phaser.io/phaser/concepts/fx) (camera-wide `postFX`, confirmed working here via `Components.PostPipeline` even though it doesn't appear directly in `Camera.js`'s own source). Root cause: Light2D's falloff (`1 - d²/r²`) holds near-full brightness across most of a light's radius and only drops away sharply right at the edge, so sizing a radius to "how far this should visibly reach" puts almost the whole visible transition into a thin ring right at that edge — a rim, not a taper. Fix, in order of what actually paid for itself: (1) radii several times larger than the visible glow's real extent (`RADIUS` in `lighting.js`) push that steep part of the curve out past where the light still matters, leaving only the gentle near-flat start on screen — free, since centre brightness is intensity-only (attenuation is always 1 at d=0); (2) that overlap between neighbours (windows a few tiles apart) is itself part of the effect, turning a row of dots into one continuous warm wash along a facade; `streetlamp`'s radius is disproportionately larger than the other two because a lamp usually has no neighbour close enough to blend its edge away, so it alone has to go soft on its own. (3) A camera-wide Bloom pass was tried and pulled back out: even at its cheapest settings it measurably cut real-time frame pacing enough to flake the walk-speed smoke checks — the same shape of regression the `maxLights` lesson above already burned once on, and GAME_SPEC's mobile target makes that cost less affordable, not more. A single cheap Vignette (`cam.postFX.addVignette`, confirmed negligible cost) stayed. Verified in real Chromium: `node tools/smoke.mjs` 64/64 stable across repeated runs (the walk-speed check is exactly what caught Bloom's cost), plus visual review at noon (unaffected), a lone streetlamp (soft merged glow, no visible rim) and a nine-window facade at night (a continuous wash, not stacked discs, and not overexposed). |
| 9  | City data schema + loader (data-driven, hand-editable map) | done | `src/game/tilemap/mapLoader.js` + `public/assets/city.json`, replacing the old hardcoded `devmap.js` (deleted). **The city is now a runtime-loaded JSON asset** (`this.load.json` in DevScene, same as any other asset), not a JS module Vite bundles in — editing `city.json` and refreshing changes the city, no code touched, no rebuild, which is the literal reading of the user's requirement. Hand-writing a 120x68 map cell by cell would make that a fiction nobody actually does, so the schema keeps devmap.js's old authoring sugar: `bands` (fill a row range), `stripes` (repeat a tile every `step` columns, e.g. a dashed centre line), sparse `cells` (one-off overrides), applied in that order per layer; `platforms`/`buildings`/`streetlamps` pass through as the already-compact structured lists the renderer wants. **Validation is the point of this system, not a nicety**: a hand-edited file is where typos live, so `loadCityMap` accumulates every error before throwing (same discipline as `tools/flat.mjs`'s `validateGrid` for sprite art) — bad row ranges, out-of-bounds building/platform/streetlamp footprints, and (given a live scene) unknown tile names, each naming the offending entry (`buildings[2].facade[3]`, not "map invalid"). Tile-name validation is checked against **the atlas the scene actually has loaded** (`scene.textures.get(TILES_KEY).has(name)`), not a second list duplicated at runtime, and is the one check skipped when no scene is passed — deliberately, so the bulk of the loader (bounds, band/stripe/cell expansion) has pure-function tests with no browser, the same testing shape as `shadowFor`/`ambientFor`/`glowFor`. **Also used to stress-test #8's lighting limits, per user request**: `city.json` extends the original 3-building block to 5 (still 120x68, using frontage the old map left empty) plus 15 streetlamps (`art/flat/tiles.mjs`'s new `LAMP_POST` feature — pole + bulb, the bulb baked pale so it reads right unlit and lets the shader do the rest; `_buildStreetlamp` in `renderer.js` is new point-placed sugar alongside platforms/buildings, registering both a shadow caster and a `streetlamp`-kind light at the bulb, `sun.js`'s third `GLOW_CURVES` entry — a sharper photocell-like on/off than windows, palest of the three colours), for 58 total light sources map-wide against a shader `maxLights` of 16. That gap is deliberate, not a bug to fix: Phaser's `LightsManager` culls to the nearest `maxLights` lights whose radius overlaps the *camera's own viewport* each frame, so a map can have as many lights as it wants — only how many are visible in one screen at once is bounded, and which ones those are moves with the camera. Verified in real Chromium: `node tools/smoke.mjs` — pure loader checks (a band fills its full range, a cell overrides a band, a stripe leaves its gaps null, buildings pass through unchanged) plus both failure modes (out-of-bounds footprint, unknown tile name) failing loudly and by name; live, the city defines far more lights (58) than the shader's cap (16), the active count never exceeds that cap at either end of the street, and the *set* of which lights are active at the west end shares zero members with the set 1720px away at the east end — the cutoff is real and camera-relative, not a fixed subset. Screenshots (`city-lights-west.png`/`-east.png`) show it directly: a lamppost in frame with its bulb dark is one that lost the cut that frame, not a broken light. **Follow-up from user review**: the streetlamp's shadow initially reused #6's box-caster model (a swept footprint rect) at the pole's own 1-tile footprint, and it read as a wide slab dragged sideways — correct for a building, which really fills its plan at every height, wrong for a pole one pixel wide. `shadows.js` gained a second caster kind, sprite casters: the shadow is the caster's own atlas frame, alpha-cut solid black (`source-in` composite) and projected with a single canvas shear+scale transform derived straight from `shadowFor`'s existing per-height offset (a pixel at local height `h` above the sprite's own ground anchor lands at `anchor + (dx,dy)*(h/heightPx)` — the same linear relationship a box caster's sweep already assumes between a footprint and its top, just applied per row instead of only at the two extremes of a rect). Box casters (buildings/platforms) are untouched — their footprint really is filled, so the sweep stays correct for them; only the streetlamp switched. Not clipped onto elevated roof/platform-top surfaces, unlike box casters — a low prop's shadow reaching a neighbour's roof isn't a case this project has hit. |
